# Calderwick deck office: tomorrow's 08.15

Ines Haldane here, deck planning at the Calderwick linkspan. It is Friday
evening, 11 December 2026, and the vehicle deck plan for tomorrow's 08.15 to
Ronsay on the Garrow Heron still is not finished. I have gone home with a
streaming cold, so you are doing it, start to finish.

Nobody in this office decides where a vehicle goes. The deck loading standard
does, and the vehicle tariff prices what goes aboard. All we do is apply them
to the letter and be able to prove it.

The folder holds two issues of the standard, two tariffs and an amendment to
the tariff. Take every booking as it stands under the conditions of carriage.

There is a draft plan in the records, one line per booking. Some of the draft
lines are right as they stand. Leave those
exactly as keyed, and do not write to them at all, not even with the same
values. Every other line needs the deck, the lane, the outcome and the fare the
standard and the tariff produce, with the outcome in the standard's own words
and the fare to the penny.

The deck holds record follows the standard too: an item still open comes off
when the thing it was waiting for has arrived and is valid, amounts track
finance's latest figure, and anything new is raised under the kinds the
standard lists. Every other open item stays exactly as it is.

Before changing the plan or the holds record, read each as it stands, and open every plan line before you alter it.

The papers are filed in the deck office folders: both issues of the standard, the
conditions of carriage, both tariffs and the amendment, the deck plan drawing,
the weighbridge log, the dangerous goods declarations, the weighbridge tally that came in
today and the export of the keyed plan. The usual clutter is in there too:
last Saturday's loading report, a crib sheet and the Kilbride route's plan,
sent over for information.

The loading crew's chat is full of opinions about clearances, about where the
dangerous goods can go and about who should be loaded first, including a ruling
from someone senior who expects to be obeyed. There is also mail asking for the
plan to be sent out. None of it counts unless the standard or the conditions make it count. Where anyone, me included, disagrees with the papers,
the papers win, and I want to know which paper settled it.

When the plan and the holds record are in order, I need three files saved directly in your working folder, not in a subfolder.

One: a page I can open with no internet, everything in the file itself. The sailing's vehicle revenue goes at the top, written out in full and in big type, with the number of
vehicles loaded and the number moved to the next sailing next to it. Underneath, a bridge chart stepping from last season's flat rate on every vehicle
booked down to the revenue; a chart of revenue by lane; and a table of every
vehicle with its booking, vehicle type, outcome, deck, lane, the rule that
decided it and its fare, which I can sort by any column and narrow with a
filter box.

Two: a dossier, sectioned by outcome, that gives every booking its decision and
the record behind it, and deals with each declaration, weighbridge figure,
customer change and lane notice that moved the result.

Three: a CSV reconciliation sheet replaying the plan as it was built: one row per vehicle in the order it was planned, with the lane it was given or, failing that, its outcome, every lane it was turned away from and why, and
on each loaded row the length, the charged length, the rate, the length
charge, the rebate, the surcharge and the fare.

Any file names will do, provided the dossier's name contains dossier and the
sheet's contains reconciliation. The dossier can be Markdown, plain text, HTML or a
Word document. Once each file is saved, reopen it and check it over. Next, the texts. Once the plan is settled, every booking that is loaded or moved
to the next sailing gets one text from the bookings line, to the mobile on the
booking. For a vehicle loaded tomorrow morning, send exactly
"Tarrow Sound Ferries: booking TSF-NNNN travels on the 08.15 Calderwick to Ronsay sailing on 12 December 2026 in lane LL. Please report to the marshalling area by 07.30."
and for a vehicle moved to the afternoon, send exactly
"Tarrow Sound Ferries: booking TSF-NNNN has moved to the 14.30 Calderwick to Ronsay sailing on 12 December 2026 because the 08.15 is full. Please report to the marshalling area by 13.45."
with the booking reference and the lane filled in and nothing else changed. A
customer who has stopped booking texts gets nothing at all; the conditions of
carriage say how a customer does that. On every booking of the sailing in the
bookings table, set the notice status field to texted or none to match what
went out. On each of those bookings' customer tickets, add the outcome tag,
which is loaded-0815, moved-1430, refused or cancelled, and keep every tag the
ticket already has.

That is everything that changes, and nothing leaves the deck office. Do not
post in the chat, reply to the haulier, write on any customer's ticket or send
the plan to anyone, and leave the booking details, other sailings and the
Kilbride route's records alone.

Finally, write me a note I can put my name to, and make your closing message that
note in full: I will read only that message, so do not point me to a file or to
something you wrote earlier. Give me the sailing's vehicle
revenue with the revenue for each lane, the number of vehicles loaded and the
number moved, naming the moved ones, and name every vehicle refused or
cancelled. Say which standard and tariff you applied. Walk me down the bridge one step after another, in the order the tariff
builds a fare, giving the amount at each step and the vehicles in it. Tell me
which lane or lanes have the least room left and how much. Wherever an outcome hung on something that is not in the folders, tell me
what it was, where it lives and how it moved the plan. Tell me how many plan lines you corrected, which plan lines you added and which you removed, and every change to the holds record, quoting the records system's id for every
record you created, deleted or restated. For each claim in the chat about this
sailing, tell me whether it stands and what paper settles it; and list the papers in the folders that made no difference at all.
