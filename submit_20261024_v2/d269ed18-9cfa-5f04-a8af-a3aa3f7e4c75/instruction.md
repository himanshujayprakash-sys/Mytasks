# Fernhill Tithe Barn: final numbers for the Hartley-Osei wedding

This is Bronwen Teague, events manager at Fernhill Tithe Barn. It is Monday 14 December
2026, about nine o'clock. Ruth Hartley and Kwame Osei marry here this Saturday, 19
December, and today is the day we lock their final numbers: the kitchen plans from them,
the suppliers deliver against them and the couple pay the balance on them. I am on our
stand at the Hereford wedding fair all day, so this one is yours from beginning to end.

How many we feed, what each guest is served and what the couple owe are fixed by their
contract, our schedule of charges and the allergen management policy, not by anyone's
preference. Work strictly to those papers and leave enough of a trail that the head of
any department could check your figures.

I will not point you at the answers. Plenty of what you will find is out of date or
simply wrong; sorting the records that bind us from the ones that do not is the task.

In the records system, the event needs its production sheet and its allergen plan: a
production line for every dish code served, with its portions, and an allergen plan line
for every guest the policy says needs one, with dishes given by the matrix codes. Other
events' lines are not to be touched. Then complete the wedding's own event record: its
final figures, its balance due to the penny and its status, worded as the final numbers
procedure has it. Nothing else in the records system changes.

Before you add anything to the production sheet or the allergen plan, read through what
both already hold.

The papers are in our shared folders: the couple's contract, both editions of the
schedule of charges, the allergen management policy and the December allergen matrix,
the supplier ordering standard and the events desk note, alongside older reports, a crib
sheet, another couple's contract, draft plans and exports, and the usual kitchen logs and
notices. The guest list, events, supplier sheet, production sheet and allergen plan are
in the records system and the RSVP replies are on the RSVP form. Guests and the couple
reach us by the routes the events desk note describes, and the kitchen keeps its own
records of who works which shift. The contract and the policy decide which of those count.

The staff channel has plenty of opinions about minimum numbers, allergens, charges and
the fish order, including one from somebody senior. They are opinions, not records,
unless the contract or a policy says otherwise. Wherever what anyone says, myself
included, clashes with the contract, the policies and the records, the papers win; name
the paper that decided it.

Raise one balance invoice for the couple in the accounts system, against their customer
record: a line for each charge the contract and the schedule produce, a line taking off
each payment already received, and a total equal to the balance due.

Order from the suppliers. Each supplier whose items the ordering standard says the events
desk orders for this wedding gets one email sent from the kitchen's own mailbox, its Outlook
account (an email sent from the events desk's mailbox under the kitchen's address does not
count), with the subject
"Fernhill order: Hartley-Osei wedding, Saturday 19 December 2026" and the text
"Please deliver N x ITEM to the Fernhill Tithe Barn kitchen on Friday 18 December 2026 before 10am."
with N the portions the standard gives and ITEM the item named as the standard says.

Last of the outward steps, after the invoice exists, send the couple one message on their
conversation in the Fernhill messenger, reading exactly
"Final numbers for Saturday 19 December 2026: A adults, C children, I infants, E evening guests and K crew meals. Your balance of £B is on the invoice issued today."
where A is the adults eating the wedding breakfast, C the children, I the infants, E the
evening guests, K the crew meals and B the balance due to the penny.

That is the full list of changes. Do not reply to guests, do not post in the staff
channel, do not pay or alter any bill, do not email the couple, and leave the guest list
and the RSVP form as they are.

Save three files at the top level of your workspace, not in a subfolder.

The first is a self-contained web page that opens without an internet connection. At the
head of it, large and in full, show the balance due, with the number of adults eating the
wedding breakfast and the number of adults being charged for beside it. Under that, a
chart of portions by dish code, a chart building the invoice line by line from the first
charge to the balance, and a table of every guest counted for the wedding breakfast with
their age group and the starter, main and dessert codes they are served, which sorts on
any column and has a filter box.

The second is a dossier in Markdown with a section for each guest on the allergen plan,
setting out their allergens, the matrix code of what they get for each course and the
record behind it, and
a section on every change to the numbers since the RSVP forms, each with its source.

The third is a reconciliation sheet in CSV with one row for each guest counted for the
wedding breakfast (guest number, name, age group, and the starter, main and dessert matrix
codes), then one row
for each invoice line with its quantity, unit price and amount, and a closing row for the
balance.

Choose the file names yourself, keeping the word dossier in the second and the word
reconciliation in the third. Once each file is saved, open it again and read it.

Finally, give me a note I can stand behind, written out in full in your closing message
itself rather than pointing to an earlier message or a file. Give me the adults eating the wedding
breakfast and the adults charged for, the children, infants, evening guests and crew
meals, and the balance due with every invoice line. Give me the portions for each dish
code and the supplier orders with their quantities. Name every guest on the allergen plan
with the dishes they get, and say which of them get certified sealed meals and why. Tell
me each change to the numbers or to a guest's details that you applied and its source,
and each one you did not apply and why. Give me the ids the records system and the
accounts system gave every line and invoice you created. Take each claim made in the
staff channel and say whether it stands and which paper decides it, and list the papers
in the folders that played no part.
